**Risani Mukhopadhyay**

*Graduate Student*

I completed my Master’s in Stem Cell Technology and Regenerative Biology from the Manipal Institute of Regenerative Medicine, MAHE. My research interests lie in understanding how early developmental events shape brain development and influence susceptibility to neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders later in life. This interest brought me to CHINTA as a Project Intern, where I investigated the effects of prenatal stress on neural development using human stem cell-derived embryoid bodies and cortical organoids. Through this work, I became increasingly fascinated by how molecular and cellular changes give rise to complex neural function and dysfunction across larger spatial and temporal scales.

As I transitioned into my PhD, I had the opportunity to explore different approaches and questions to understand the functioning of the brain. I am now a member of the Navigation and Neurosimulation Lab, where I hope to explore how neural and glial signalling emerge from interactions across molecular, cellular, and network levels by developing computational models that bridge these scales and provide deeper insights into brain function and behaviour.

Outside the laboratory, I enjoy reading, travelling, cooking and baking, and spending time with friends and family. Having grown up across different cultures and countries, I have always been curious about new places, perspectives, and ways of thinking. These experiences have shaped how I approach both life and science, fostering an appreciation for connections across disciplines and scales. In the long term, I hope to contribute to a more integrated understanding of the brain by combining experimental and computational approaches to neuroscience.
